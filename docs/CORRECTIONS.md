# Batch 1 verification, 3 September 2026

## Reversal: Hammer

I was wrong. The court's Notice to the Bar of 1 September 2026 states that
Judge Silagi fills "the full-time magistrate judgeship position vacated upon the
retirement of Magistrate Judge Michael A. Hammer." Hammer joined Lowenstein
Sandler on 2 September 2026 after fifteen years on the bench, having been
appointed 25 July 2011 and reappointed in 2019. He served as chief magistrate
judge.

The prior research report was right to carry Silagi and not Hammer. My earlier
correction, which added Hammer as a sitting judge on the strength of the Our
Judges page, was wrong. The directory is stale. It still lists Hammer and does
not yet list Silagi.

The operating lesson is worth more than the fix. No single source is current.
The chambers directory lags; the Notices to the Bar lead. A roster check that
reads one page and stops will be wrong in both directions. Reconcile both,
every time. Hammer's record is retained as a former judge so that opinions he
authored carry a correct attribution, and the poison list now blocks any
description of him as sitting.

The site would have published a vacated opinion as the governing appellate

# Batch 2 verification, 3 September 2026

All four flagship matters now carry a stated current posture. Three of the four
are suspended, and none of the three is suspended in the way the prior report
implies.

## ANJRPC

The Third Circuit's order of 4 August 2026, by Judge Freeman, stays issuance of
the mandate pending the Supreme Court's disposition of New Jersey's anticipated
certiorari petition or the expiration of the time to file; if certiorari is
granted the stay continues through final disposition on the merits. New Jersey
moved on 31 July, which itself suspended issuance while the motion was pending.
No petition has been filed. The deadline is 15 October 2026, extendable by
application to the Circuit Justice.

The distinction that has to survive into the summary is that the stay runs
against the mandate, not the opinion. The holding stands as circuit precedent
while the relief is suspended and the statutes remain enforceable. Say that
precisely or say nothing.

## Khalil

Rehearing en banc was denied 6-5 on 22 May 2026, over a dissent by Judges
Krause, Restrepo and Freeman. The mandate was stayed on 26 May under Rule 41(d);
without the stay it would have issued on 29 May and the district court's orders
would have dissolved. No certiorari petition is confirmed filed. Separately the
Board of Immigration Appeals has entered a final order of removal, and review of
that order runs through a petition for review in another circuit, not through
this case.

Two things must both be true in any published sentence: the Third Circuit never
reached the constitutional merits, and its vacatur has not taken effect. The
temptation is to write one and drop the other.

## McIver

Judge Semper denied dismissal as to two counts on 13 November 2025 and reserved
judgment on the third, then declined to dismiss that count in January 2026.
McIver appealed both rulings. On 26 August 2026 the Third Circuit affirmed as to
Counts One and Two under the two-step test of United States v. Menendez, vacated
and remanded Count Three, and dismissed the remainder for lack of jurisdiction.
Judge Ambro would have vacated as to Count Two and permitted discovery on the
retaliation claim.

Count Three asserts no specific actus reus and reaches a broader range of
conduct than the district court analyzed; the remand asks whether that conduct
includes legislative acts and, if so, whether the count survives once protected
conduct is removed. Counts One and Two may proceed. No trial date is set; trial
was adjourned indefinitely in November 2025.

The count numbering does not reconcile across sources. One repository copy of
the November order reads as a denial on Counts One and Three with judgment
reserved on Count Two, while the appellate opinion and contemporaneous reporting
treat Count Three as the reserved and remanded count. Read the signed order.
Do not take count numbers from press coverage.

## Gate change

Batch 2 produced a new rule. A correct appellate history is not sufficient,
because a reader who sees "vacated" or "stayed" still does not know what governs
today. Gate 6a now requires that any opinion with a disturbed posture carry a
`current_posture` statement with its own as-of date, refreshed on the same
window as the status checks. Stayed mandates are the specific trap: three of the
four flagship matters have one, and in each the naive reading is wrong in a
different direction.
